**2. Top 5 (optional, not used for elimination).**

2. **C09 — Viewer plants it, the streamer sets it off:** Viewer baiting creates an ongoing social game with a strong visual payoff.

**3. Notes (optional).**

- **Q2:** I treated the stated demo views as recordable. A visibly translated message, filtered question, or phone interaction can be understood without narration even when the actual tool is private. None requires a fatal design judgment at its best presentation.
- **Best variants:** C22 receives its technical pass for the emote-ammo game; C27 passes innovation for regulars’ introductions; C33 passes for the streamer clearing viewer-generated falling blocks by voice.
- **C01:** The chatter list represents accounts connected to chat, not a reliable list of people watching silently. It cannot establish the claimed lurker presence.
- **C09 and C18:** Deleting a posted message does not make its original delivery secret. The streamer or other viewers may see it before deletion; C18’s anti-copying claim is therefore unreliable.
- **C20 and C30:** I would not assume a universal Twitch “24-hour data rule” without identifying the applicable requirement. Link expiration also does not erase downloaded copies.
- **C23:** Removing a message from public chat after delivery is different from holding it before anyone sees it. The private streamer-view variant avoids that distinction.
- **C17 and C31:** These substantially overlap as attributed message queues with streamer-controlled presentation, although their admission rules differ.
