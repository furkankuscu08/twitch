## A. Crowd map

What most of the 565 are likely to ship, given the organizer examples:

1. Chat votes the next move in a game ("Twitch Plays").
2. A live poll or prediction overlay with A/B/C in chat.
3. A dice, bingo, or trivia overlay for TTRPG and variety streams.
4. An AI co-host or "chat, summarized" widget.
5. A skinned alert pack: follow, raid, sub, with a custom animation.
6. Viewer-paid or viewer-triggered sounds and on-stream distractions.
7. A "clip that" button that turns chat spam into a highlight list.
8. A Nightbot-style command bot with a pretty overlay.
9. Merged chat or a shared scene for two streamers collaborating.
10. A sports, race, or IRL score bug fed by chat or a manual hotkey.

## C. Five full cards

### Raid Gate
- One-liner: The raid is held on the streamer's side until they see whether the destination chat can actually talk back.
- Who installs it / who gets the value (streamer, viewers, mods — separately): The streamer installs it and is the one who gains the veto. Their viewers get a one-line reason if the jump is held, so they aren't dumped into silence. Mods do nothing.
- Streamer category fit: Anyone who raids out. Strongest for small and mid channels who raid peers at the end of stream. lindahgxt already filters raids to smaller same-category channels that are not follower-only ([11 Jul 2026](https://x.com/lindahgxt/status/2075763319932334386)).
- Core loop (what the viewer does → what happens on screen): The streamer chooses a channel. The overlay shows live / title / follower-only / follow-age if the API returns it, and two buttons: Raid, or Pick someone else. Nothing is sent until Raid. Their own viewers see that card for a few seconds, then it leaves.
- First 10 seconds of the demo (what a judge sees): A test channel is set to follower-only with a 10-minute wait. The streamer hits raid. A small card says the destination requires a 10-minute follow before anyone can speak. They press Pick someone else. The raid never starts. Cut to a second target with open chat, press Raid, and Twitch's normal raid fires.
- Twitch surfaces used: Helix chat settings (read the target), Helix start raid only after confirm, Get Streams for live/title. EventSub `channel.raid` is optional, for a later incoming toast. OBS browser source for the card. No extension.
- Works with 1 streamer + 3 test accounts? How? Yes. Follower-only is a setting on a channel; the target does not have to be live for the hold to be shown (inference: chat settings are readable off-stream). The hero shot is the blocked raid, so the demo needs one live channel: the builder's. A successful raid needs a second live channel for about a minute; if that isn't available, the video ends on the hold, which is the product.
- Closest existing thing and the meaningful difference: Twitch's own raid screen sometimes hints at follower-only, and annnub's workaround is to click the target's chat box first. Streamer.bot can script around raids (unknown whether anyone has already shipped this exact check). Raid Gate's whole screen is that decision, it includes the follow-age when the API provides it, and Start Raid is not called until the streamer confirms.
- Why wouldn't 30 other teams build this? The organizer examples point at games, polls, and dice. A pre-raid veto looks like "alerts," which already have owners, so most teams will skip it. The 2024 gallery the brief describes was dice, polls, bingo, trivia, and AI chat. A judge who has sat through those still has a picture: the raid that did not happen, and the reason on screen.
- Evidence it links to (section 5 items), or "unvalidated — how we'd validate": Brief item on raids as a handoff [V]. X, verified: TamaraLuvss (the wish, 315 likes), annnub (the manual workaround), OMJess_ (the 10-minute wait), NosAsrai1 (they leave, and so do the people they brought). Also [@FrozenBox, 12 Mar 2026](https://x.com/FrozenBox/status/2031904260439376293): "something that may be holding you back is the follower only chat. I gotta wait 10 minutes before I can say anything." [@BlueRivule, 2 Jul 2026](https://x.com/BlueRivule/status/2072523199175184388): "Twitch needs to implement raid bypass for follower only chat." One counterpoint, so title and uptime are shown and do not block: a starting-soon raid made curlylocksgamer's night.
- MVP scope / explicitly out of scope: One overlay card, one settings read, one confirm, one start-raid call. In: follower-only boolean. In if the field exists: follow-age minutes. Out: picking a raid target for them, raid trains, auto-messaging the destination, changing the destination's settings, a BRB blocker, shoutouts.
- Riskiest assumption: That chat settings for a channel we do not moderate are readable with our token, including `follower_mode_duration`. Inference from public Helix behavior, not called in this session. The brief lists "chat settings" and "raids (start/cancel)" but not this field or this authorization case. First hour of the build is that one call against a channel we don't mod. If it 403s, the fallback is anonymous chat `ROOMSTATE`, which publishes `followers-only=` in minutes — unknown whether Twitch still offers that in Oct 2026, and it sits outside the brief's surface list. If both fail, this card moves to Parked.

### One Line
Familiar base: alert and chat overlays.
- One-liner: A streamer with one monitor gets a single line over the game, and the rest of chat stays off that screen.
- Who installs it / who gets the value (streamer, viewers, mods — separately): The streamer installs a small always-on-top window on their own desktop. Viewers are unaffected unless the streamer also mirrors that one line as a thin browser source. Mods are unaffected. Lurkers never appear.
- Streamer category fit: One-PC gaming, IRL, and anyone who alt-tabs to read chat. Art and focus streams are a worse fit than Three Lines; this one is for people mid-match.
- Core loop (what the viewer does → what happens on screen): A first message this stream, or any line with a question mark, becomes the one line on the streamer's window. Anything else is dropped. The streamer glances, answers in their own chat client or hits a key to dismiss. The slot clears. A second qualifier waits its turn. The window is click-through so it doesn't steal the game.
- First 10 seconds of the demo (what a judge sees): Fullscreen gameplay, no second monitor in the shot. Test account asks "what build is that?" The line appears over the corner of the game. The streamer answers out loud and presses dismiss. A second account says "lol" and nothing appears. A third account, first message, says "hi" and the line changes to that hello.
- Twitch surfaces used: EventSub `channel.chat.message`. Optional Helix send only if the streamer taps a canned reply they wrote themselves. Optional tiny OBS browser source echoing the same one line, so the Twitch video shows it too. The real fix is the local window: an OBS overlay is not visible to a fullscreen one-monitor player.
- Works with 1 streamer + 3 test accounts? How? Yes. Three chatters are the whole demo. "First message" means first this session, so nothing is stored after the stream ends.
- Closest existing thing and the meaningful difference: A full chat overlay, and products that sell one specifically for the missing second monitor. [@streamwidget, 4 Aug 2026](https://x.com/streamwidget/status/2084693366508552690): "No second monitor, no tabbing away mid-sentence," pulling Twitch, YouTube, Kick, and TikTok into one feed. [@SuzyPlayz, 23 Oct 2026](https://x.com/SuzyPlayz/status/2113744907722690672) has streamed on one monitor for two years and uses spoken alerts ("New Follower"), which covers alerts and leaves chat unread. One Line shows one qualifying sentence and refuses the feed.
- Why wouldn't 30 other teams build this? The obvious build is a prettier full chat. An almost empty window looks under-designed in a screenshot. The demo is the empty moment: "lol" produces nothing, the question produces one line. That is the memory test against five alert packs.
- Evidence it links to: Need #1 [V in the brief]. X, verified: HitoriHaven (chat in game, no second monitor). [@MissusBonk, 23 Oct 2026](https://x.com/MissusBonk/status/2113603590497452480): "since I have no second monitor I have to use my phone for chat and tab out of games." [@eepydemxn, 22 Jul 2026](https://x.com/eepydemxn/status/2079725523127652691): "ion have a camera or a second monitor so i prob can't read chat sorry." Need #9 (don't cover the game) is why it is one line.
- MVP scope / explicitly out of scope: One window, two filters (question mark, first message this session), dismiss hotkey. Out: multi-platform chat, summaries, TTS, a lurker prompt, history longer than the session, emote rendering.
- Riskiest assumption: A click-through always-on-top window stays usable over a fullscreen game on Windows without being captured into the stream by accident, and without feeling like the overlay-that-covers-40% problem. The broadcast echo has to stay a single row or it fails need #9. Unknown until it's on a real fullscreen game for ten minutes.

### Blind Minute
- One-liner: One key closes text chat for a timed scene, leaves emotes open, and puts the old settings back by itself.
- Who installs it / who gets the value (streamer, viewers, mods — separately): The streamer installs it and arms it. Viewers feel it immediately in chat: words fail, emotes work, and a small on-stream badge says how long is left. Mods don't have to type a public command. When it expires, the previous follower-only / slow / emote state returns, which is the part people forget.
- Streamer category fit: Story games, horror, puzzles, first playthroughs. Weak for shooters and just chatting.
- Core loop (what the viewer does → what happens on screen): Streamer presses Blind. Overlay shows "Blind 0:45 — emotes only," in the streamer's words, one chat announcement. A viewer types "he's behind the door" and Twitch rejects it. They send an emote and it lands. The timer ends. Words work again, and a 10-minute follower rule that was already on is still on.
- First 10 seconds of the demo (what a judge sees): Streamer: "Don't tell me the code." They hit the key. Test account's spoiler does not appear. Test account's emote does. The badge counts down in the corner. Cut, or wait, and the same account's sentence goes through after expiry.
- Twitch surfaces used: Helix chat settings, read then update `emote_mode`, restore on a timer. One Send Chat Message when it arms. OBS browser source for the badge. EventSub is unnecessary for the MVP.
- Works with 1 streamer + 3 test accounts? How? Yes. One account tries the spoiler, one sends an emote, one waits and speaks after the timer. No crowd, no channel points.
- Closest existing thing and the meaningful difference: The emote-only switch already in Twitch's dashboard, and a rules popup. Calamity Panda already has the popup, and the first-time chatter backseats anyway. Blind Minute is a physical close on text, with an expiry, so a small channel doesn't leave emote-only on for the rest of the night (SkyeKatty's complaint).
- Why wouldn't 30 other teams build this? It looks too small to be a hackathon project: "you wrapped a checkbox." The part worth filming is the restore. Teams chasing the organizer examples will build a distraction button, which is the opposite direction (viewers push effects in; this keeps a hint out for one minute).
- Evidence it links to: Need #5, bot spam, is the risk this card has to avoid, so it announces once and then gets out of the way [V in the brief]. X, verified: CalamityPandaVT (rules popup, first-time chatter backseats anyway). lion_mx1 (streamers reduced to asking for smiley faces). SkyeKatty (these modes are supposed to be temporary, especially for small creators).
- MVP scope / explicitly out of scope: One hotkey, one duration, save and restore of the chat-settings object, one badge, one announcement. Out: detecting which message was a spoiler, a hint drawer, deleting messages, per-user exceptions, sub-only.
- Riskiest assumption: Inference — a moderator token on a non-affiliate channel can read and write emote-only, and the settings object can be written back unchanged. The brief lists chat settings and does not mark them affiliate-only. Unknown until the first write. Second risk: viewers experience the minute as the bot silencing them. The badge and the auto-restore are the mitigation; if a playtest with the three test accounts feels punitive, the card fails the design criterion even though the API works.

### Three Lines
Familiar base: live polls / Q&A.
- One-liner: While the streamer's hands are busy, up to three questions stay on screen in the viewer's own words until one is pulled.
- Who installs it / who gets the value (streamer, viewers, mods — separately): The streamer installs it. A viewer who asked something gets a visible waiting spot, so a slow reply doesn't look like a snub. Lurkers are never prompted and never listed. Mods can clear a line from a private page; they don't need a public `!command`.
- Streamer category fit: Art, music, ranked games, boss fights, IRL with no free hand. Poor fit for a chat that already moves faster than three lines.
- Core loop (what the viewer does → what happens on screen): A viewer sends a line ending in `?`, or starting with `!ask`. If fewer than three are up, it joins the stack, verbatim, with the name. The streamer, between attempts, presses a key to pull the top one: it enlarges, the other two stay, and the pulled one leaves after they answer. No bot reply on submit. Optional: the pulled line is also sent to Twitch's pinned chat.
- First 10 seconds of the demo (what a judge sees): The streamer is drawing or in a menu. Test account: "what's the brush?" It appears as line one. Second account: "lol" — no change. Third account: "how long did the sketch take?" Line two. The streamer pulls line one, reads it aloud, and it clears. Line two is now on top.
- Twitch surfaces used: EventSub chat, OBS browser source (three lines, small, corner), streamer hotkey on a local page. Pinned Chat API only as a bonus on the pulled line. Helix send is out of the default path so this doesn't become a welcome bot.
- Works with 1 streamer + 3 test accounts? How? Yes. It is better with three people than with three hundred, because the failure it fixes is one missed hello, not a flood. Session-only storage.
- Closest existing thing and the meaningful difference: Nightbot quote/question commands, YouTube Live's Q&A mode (the brief marks Twitch as having no native equivalent [V]), and a poll. A poll asks the room to pick a winner. Three Lines keeps the asker's sentence and gives the streamer a turn order. It does not greet, it does not summarize, and it does not speak on its own.
- Why wouldn't 30 other teams build this? Many will build a poll, and a judge who has seen five polls will only remember this if the demo is the MagicTeaHouse scene: the message stays up, unanswered, until a human pulls it, and a non-question never enters. If the video looks like a poll, it fails the memory test. The script has to show "lol" being ignored.
- Evidence it links to: Needs #1 and #6 [V in the brief]. YouTube Q&A gap [V in the brief]. X, verified: MagicTeaHouseVT (the missed new chatter who leaves). harukami_2277 (focus mode, forgets chat exists, which is why they don't stream). [@BetTheSelf, 5 Jun 2026](https://x.com/BetTheSelf/status/2063026781288902716): "I tried to go live and answer questions but I can't see chat."
- MVP scope / explicitly out of scope: Three slots, `?` and `!ask`, pull, dismiss, clear. Out: upvotes, LLM rewriting, TTS, auto-welcome, more than three, cross-stream history, pinning if the new API misbehaves.
- Riskiest assumption: That a viewer who can see their own sentence waiting will stay through 2–30 seconds of stream delay plus however long the hands are busy. MagicTeaHouse's chatter left on a perceived snub; a visible queue is the hypothesis, and it is unvalidated until one art-stream playtest. If people still leave, the card is only a streamer convenience and the impact score drops.

### Ten Seconds
- One-liner: A mod on a phone confirms a timeout, then has ten seconds to undo it, with buttons far enough apart to miss.
- Who installs it / who gets the value (streamer, viewers, mods — separately): The streamer mods the bot. Mods open a private web page on their phone; they get the confirm step and the undo. The timed-out viewer gets their timeout reversed when the mod hits undo, without a public `!unban` performance. The streamer can use the same page but isn't the main user.
- Streamer category fit: Any channel with at least one mod who isn't sitting at the dashboard. Small channels included; the page is the same at 4 viewers.
- Core loop (what the viewer does → what happens on screen): A mod sees recent chat on the phone page. Timeout is a large button, then a second confirm. After it lands, a full-width Undo stays for ten seconds and calls unban. The on-stream picture barely changes: the point of the demo video is the phone next to the chat, the mistaken timeout, and the user still talking two seconds later.
- First 10 seconds of the demo (what a judge sees): Test account says something harmless. A thumb hits Timeout, then Confirm. The account's next message fails. The mod hits Undo before the bar empties. The same account speaks again and the message lands. A second button, Ban, sits far from Timeout.
- Twitch surfaces used: EventSub chat for the phone list. Helix ban and unban (inference: standard moderator endpoints; the brief's Helix list does not name them, and they are not described as affiliate-gated). Private web page, not an Extension and not an on-stream overlay.
- Works with 1 streamer + 3 test accounts? How? Yes. One account is the mod (or the streamer acting as mod), one is the accidental target, one keeps chatting so it's obvious the room wasn't frozen. The bot must be a moderator, which a new channel can do.
- Closest existing thing and the meaningful difference: Twitch's mobile mod view, which is the thing SponsordWarlock says caused the mis-ban, plus public `!timeout` commands (need #4 [V in the brief]). Ten Seconds adds distance between buttons, a confirm, and a short undo. It does not add new punishment types.
- Why wouldn't 30 other teams build this? Mod tools aren't in the organizer examples, and "fun to watch" is the weakest criterion here: the video is a phone, not a character on stream. Teams optimizing for a booth crowd will build a game. A judge who mods, or who has banned the wrong person, will remember the undo. That's a narrower memory than Raid Gate.
- Evidence it links to: Need #4 [V in the brief]. X, verified: SponsordWarlock (adjacent buttons, chat not reloading, accidental ban, they apologized in DMs). [@ManyaBerk, 25 Oct 2026](https://x.com/ManyaBerk/status/2114222174185644095) is a second mobile-mod pain, a different bug: streams pausing on the phone while they try to do mod duties. The undo-timer itself is an inference from the mis-ban post, not a second person asking for undo.
- MVP scope / explicitly out of scope: Recent chat, timeout with confirm, ten-second unban, ban placed elsewhere on the screen. Out: appeals, automod word lists, message deletion, whisper logs, a public mod theater on the overlay.
- Riskiest assumption: Inference — ban and unban succeed for a moderator bot on a brand-new channel within a second, so the undo is real and not a UI fiction. Unknown until the first call. Design risk sits beside it: if the demo video is dull, this loses on "fun to watch" even when the undo works.

## D. Parked

- **Personal "block this word" for a viewer** ([@mynamessavvy, 30 Nov 2025](https://x.com/mynamessavvy/status/1994919762338456010): hide messages containing a word, blocking the user isn't enough). Needs a client surface. Would need to be true: an extension review faster than the 5–14 day filter, or the viewer runs a userscript, which is not the hackathon demo.
- **Channel-point battles, gift effects, prediction games as the core.** Would need to be true: points and predictions work on a brand-new non-affiliate channel, and the demo still reads with three chatters. The brief says the May 2026 change is untested against docs that still say affiliate-only, so this can only be a bonus on another idea.
- **Hype train, ads, subs, charity.** Would need to be true: those products exist on the demo channel. The brief says they do not.
- **A searchable VOD memory.** Would need to be true: keeping transcripts or VOD audio past 24 hours is allowed. It isn't, under the filter used here.
- **Frame-matched rhythm or "click what you see on the video" game.** Would need to be true: viewers can act on a frame inside the 2–30 second delay. They can't. Chat-timed games stay in the crowd map instead.
- **Fixing Twitch's own caption track.** [@AuroralVisage, 27 Sep 2026](https://x.com/AuroralVisage/status/2104255367697887709) has a plugin that looks alive in OBS while Twitch says captions are unavailable, and the Stream CC button won't start. Would need to be true: we can write the caption metadata Twitch actually accepts, in a demo, without shipping audio to a cloud model. A burned-in caption row also fights need #9. A local-only subtitle line stays on the wide list, not here, and only if audio never leaves the machine and is discarded continuously.
- **AI co-host that speaks as the talent.** Would need to be true: it never takes the streamer's voice. The brief's red line is AI replacing the streamer. That constraint removes the idea rather than shaping it.

## E. Your pick

Build **Raid Gate**. The pain is a single moment several people described in plain language: you are about to hand your community to someone, and you cannot tell if those people will be allowed to say hello, especially under a 10-minute follow. Tamara's post is the wish, annnub's is the clumsy workaround, OMJess's is the 10-minute detail, and Nos's is the consequence (they leave, and they take the raid with them). The demo is one card and a raid that does not fire, which a judge understands without a crowd and without running the repo. It uses the surfaces the brief already cleared (chat settings, start/cancel raid, a small overlay), it does not need affiliate features, an extension, stored video, or an LLM, and it is aimed at small channels because raiding is how those channels move people. One Line and Three Lines are real, and they resemble the chat widgets the 2024 gallery was already full of. Blind Minute is the right second build if the settings read against another channel 403s. Ten Seconds matters to mods and is the weakest thing to watch. Raid Gate is the one that still reads after a judge has seen five polls.
