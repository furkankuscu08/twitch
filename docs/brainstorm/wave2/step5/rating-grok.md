**2. Top 5.**

**R19 Viewer plants it, the streamer sets it off (17).** Chat plants words in secret, steers the conversation, and the streamer's own mouth sets off the mine. The video is one beat a judge can retell: the word, the boom, the planter's name, the earlier bait. Weakest criterion is innovation: the pleasure is the familiar forbidden-word game, restaged with a sealed plant and an auto clip.

**R06 Something travels with the raid (16).** The torch is the idea a judge would still describe after a stack of alert packs and co-hosts. Emote rings accumulate on one channel and fly into the next, so the video has a handoff and the build is shared state across two EventSub clients. Weakest criterion is impact: the object appears when both channels are running the tool, which limits it to raid pairs and trains.

**R11 Viewers write the brief, the streamer performs it (16).** Weather, footsteps, and a surprise land on screen as a score, then paper and a cup become rain and thunder. That live performance is a different loop from playback sound alerts, and the absurd thunderclap is a moment worth replaying. Weakest criterion is technological implementation: the working piece is a role-and-cue board, so the Codex story is thinner than the speech, replay, and two-channel entries.

**R20 Production problems reach the streamer (16).** A dead mic is the failure small streamers already hear about too late, and this gives the audience a private line that still reads on camera. Chat piles up with "MIC??", the lamp goes red, the streamer unmutes, the spotter gets a credit. Weakest criterion is technological implementation: the pipeline is a 60-second keyword threshold plus a local cue, a short build next to replay or speech.

**R12 Chat-called replay review (16).** The paper ball, the VAR stinger, the rim in slow motion, the named jury, and the "NO BASKET" stamp are a full broadcast package inside the first seconds. Local replay, a timed vote, and a titled clip give the technical score something real, and the bit works on desk chaos as well as games. Weakest criterion is impact: people will call for it after a disputed beat; it is a highlight segment rather than something the channel needs every hour.

**3. Bottom 5.**

**R18 Raid lands at a bad moment.** A reminder that stays until the streamer taps "thanks" and sends the shoutout is a thin overlay on raid alerts and auto-shoutout bots.

**R16 Lurker presence made visible.** Silent viewers as dim lights is a viewer-count skin, and a single flare is too small a change to carry the video.

**R07 Bookmark a moment for later.** A break-time encore list restates stream markers and "clip that" with a credit line.

**R14 Maker continuity check.** Tagged observations plus a stage stamp serve a narrow maker checkpoint and play as a checklist.

**R09 Sealed predictions.** Honest sealed guesses are a real tweak, and the on-screen open is clear, but native Predictions already own this loop and the build is a form plus a flip.

**4. Notes.**

R01 and R09 use the same machine — whisper or web form, hidden text, envelopes on screen — for different games (tiered hints versus a simultaneous prediction reveal). They are scored as separate ideas.

R04 and R18 both sit on raids and solve different problems: briefing arrivals versus not missing the thank-you. R07 and R12 both return to a past moment; one is a deliberate encore, the other is a dispute review.
