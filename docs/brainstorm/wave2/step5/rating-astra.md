**2. Top 5.**

1. **R19 — Viewer plants it, the streamer sets it off.** The audience secretly setting traps inside a live conversation is distinctive, and the bait-to-explosion sequence gives the video a memorable payoff. Impact and Technical tie as its weakest criteria: it needs a willing conversational performer, and speech recognition must trigger reliably without accidental detonations.

2. **R20 — Production problems reach the streamer.** It solves a problem judges can recognize immediately, while the repair-and-confirm variant demonstrates that the problem actually got resolved. Innovation is a joint weakest criterion because the alert components are familiar; the distinction comes from connecting reports, private notification and confirmation.

3. **R12 — Chat-called replay review.** It can look like a finished broadcast feature within one short scene, with replay control, voting, graphics and clip creation all visibly doing useful work. Innovation is its weakest criterion because instant replay and audience adjudication are established concepts, even if this combination is unusually complete.

4. **R03 — Per-viewer souvenir moment.** The viewer requests something, the streamer performs for them, and a tangible keepsake appears: the entire value proposition fits into seconds. Innovation is its weakest criterion because photo booths and personalized snapshots are familiar, but the stream interaction and end-of-stream collage give them a strong setting.

5. **R01 — Tiered hints.** It turns the tension between knowledgeable viewers and a blind-playthrough streamer into a cooperative interaction with explicit boundaries. Technical is its weakest criterion because the strongest achievable implementation is principally a private submission queue, access controls and a polished reveal.

**3. Bottom 5.**

1. **R16 — Lurker presence made visible.** It offers a mostly decorative payoff while suggesting a level of knowledge about silent viewers that chat connection data cannot provide.
2. **R17 — Spoiler-hold filter.** Its strongest benefit is invisible prevention, making both classification quality and entertainment value difficult to establish in a short video.
3. **R08 — Newcomer welcome and orientation.** Even its most watchable variant is fundamentally a first-chatter alert, with limited evidence that the extra ceremony improves orientation.
4. **R18 — Raid lands at a bad moment.** The practical fix is credible, but its narrow workflow and familiar alert presentation give judges little to remember.
5. **R07 — Bookmark a moment for later.** A reminder list followed by an encore lacks a distinctive interaction or consistently compelling visual reveal.

**4. Notes (optional).**

- **R06:** The claim that Twitch categorically forbids retaining all Twitch data beyond 24 hours is too broad; retention requirements depend on the data and applicable conditions. I did not deduct points for this note.
- **R16:** Accounts connected to chat are not equivalent to viewers watching the stream: the list can include bots and people with chat open but no video, while missing actual viewers.
- **R02 and R07** overlap in saving viewer contributions for a later break, but their capture rules and intended payoffs are sufficiently different to score separately.
- Totals are ordered using Innovation, then Impact, then Technical, then Design to break ties. No criterion receives more than four 5s or has more than ten candidates sharing one score.
